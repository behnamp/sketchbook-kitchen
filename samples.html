Brown Butter Chocolate Chunk

Browning the butter turns it nutty and deep, like toffee. Chopped bar chocolate melts into puddles that bagged chips never will.

Makes 12 cookies · 175°C / 350°F · 11–13 min

Unsalted butter170 g
Dark brown sugar165 g
White sugar50 g
Egg50 g
Egg yolk20 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Plain flour250 g
Baking soda4 g
Fine salt4 g
Dark chocolate, chopped200 g
Flaky saltto finish
  1. Melt the butter in a light-coloured pan over medium heat. Keep stirring until it foams, the solids turn golden brown and it smells of hazelnuts. Pour into a bowl, scraping in the brown bits, and cool for 15 minutes.
  2. Whisk both sugars into the butter for 1 minute. Whisk in the egg, yolk and vanilla until glossy.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking soda and salt together, then fold them in until just combined. Fold in the chocolate.
  4. Cover and chill the dough for 1 hour. Heat the oven and line two trays with baking paper.
  5. Scoop 75 g balls, 8 cm apart. Bake until the edges are golden and the centres look slightly underdone. Sprinkle with flaky salt and cool 10 minutes on the tray.

Baker's note: The butter loses about 25 g of water as it browns. That is intended, so don't top it up.

Classic Chocolate Chip

The everyday cookie: crisp at the rim, soft in the middle, quick enough to make on a weeknight. Creaming the butter gives it a lighter crumb.

Makes 12 cookies · 190°C / 375°F · 9–11 min

Unsalted butter, soft115 g
Light brown sugar110 g
White sugar50 g
Egg50 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Plain flour190 g
Baking soda3 g
Fine salt3 g
Semisweet chocolate chips170 g
  1. Beat the butter and both sugars for 2 minutes until pale and fluffy.
  2. Beat in the egg and vanilla, scraping down the bowl.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking soda and salt, add them to the bowl and mix on low until no dry flour remains. Stir in the chips.
  4. Chill for 30 minutes if you have time; the cookies spread less and taste richer.
  5. Scoop 55 g balls onto lined trays, 6 cm apart. Bake until golden at the edges and just set in the centre. Cool 5 minutes on the tray.

Baker's note: For bakery-crinkled edges, lift the tray and drop it on the counter once, 2 minutes before the end.

Double Chocolate

Cocoa in the dough and two kinds of chocolate in the mix. Dutch-process cocoa gives the darkest colour and the smoothest taste.

Makes 12 cookies · 175°C / 350°F · 10–11 min

Unsalted butter, soft115 g
Light brown sugar120 g
White sugar50 g
Egg50 g
Vanilla extract4 g
Plain flour150 g
Dutch-process cocoa35 g
Baking soda3 g
Fine salt3 g
Milk chocolate chips100 g
Dark chocolate chips100 g
  1. Beat the butter and sugars for 2 minutes. Beat in the egg and vanilla.
  2. Sift the flour, cocoa, baking soda and salt into the bowl and mix on low until combined.
  3. Stir in both chocolates. Cover and chill for 30 minutes.
  4. Scoop 60 g balls onto lined trays, 6 cm apart.
  5. Bake until the edges are firm and the tops have just lost their shine. They will look soft; they firm up as they cool.

Baker's note: Dark cookies hide their doneness. Trust the clock and the edges, not the colour.

Oatmeal Raisin

Plump raisins, a hint of cinnamon and nutmeg, and a chewy oat base. Soaking the raisins keeps them juicy in the oven.

Makes 14 cookies · 175°C / 350°F · 11–13 min

Raisins120 g
Unsalted butter, soft115 g
Light brown sugar150 g
White sugar25 g
Egg50 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Plain flour125 g
Ground cinnamon3 g
Ground nutmeg1 g
Baking soda3 g
Fine salt3 g
Rolled oats150 g
  1. Cover the raisins with hot water for 10 minutes, drain and pat them dry.
  2. Beat the butter and sugars for 2 minutes. Beat in the egg and vanilla.
  3. Whisk the flour, spices, baking soda and salt, then mix them in on low. Stir in the oats and raisins.
  4. Chill for 30 minutes.
  5. Scoop 55 g balls onto lined trays, flatten them slightly and bake until golden around the edges. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes.

Baker's note: Swap the raisins for dried cranberries or chopped dates without changing anything else.

Vanilla Bean

The cupcake everything else is measured against: a tender, buttery crumb and a cloud of vanilla buttercream. Use a real vanilla pod if you can.

Makes 12 cupcakes · 175°C / 350°F · 18–20 min

Unsalted butter, soft115 g
White sugar200 g
Eggs, room temperature100 g
Vanilla extract6 g
Plain flour190 g
Baking powder7 g
Fine salt2 g
Milk, room temperature120 g
Vanilla buttercream
Unsalted butter, soft225 g
Powdered sugar, sifted360 g
Milk or cream30 g
Vanilla extract6 g
Vanilla bean seeds1 pod
Fine salta pinch
  1. Heat the oven and line a 12-hole muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar for 3 minutes until pale and fluffy. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking powder and salt. Add to the bowl in three parts, alternating with the milk, mixing on low only until combined.
  4. Divide the batter between the liners, about 60 g each. Bake until the tops spring back and a skewer comes out with moist crumbs. Cool for 5 minutes, then move to a rack and cool completely.
  5. For the buttercream, beat the butter for 2 minutes, add the powdered sugar in three parts, then the milk, flavouring and salt. Beat for 3 more minutes until light, then pipe a tall swirl on each cupcake.

Baker's note: Scrape the vanilla seeds into the frosting and add the empty pod to your sugar jar to make vanilla sugar.

Everyday Chocolate

Moist, deeply chocolatey and made in one bowl. Hot coffee wakes up the cocoa without making it taste of coffee.

Makes 12 cupcakes · 175°C / 350°F · 18–20 min

Plain flour130 g
Cocoa powder40 g
White sugar200 g
Baking soda5 g
Baking powder2 g
Fine salt2 g
Egg, room temperature50 g
Buttermilk120 g
Vegetable oil60 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Hot coffee or water120 g
Chocolate buttercream
Unsalted butter, soft200 g
Powdered sugar, sifted300 g
Cocoa powder50 g
Milk or cream45 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Fine salta pinch
  1. Heat the oven and line a 12-hole muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. Whisk the flour, cocoa, sugar, baking soda, baking powder and salt in a large bowl.
  3. Whisk in the egg, buttermilk, oil and vanilla, then stir in the hot coffee. The batter will be thin, which is right.
  4. Divide the batter between the liners, about 60 g each. Bake until the tops spring back and a skewer comes out with moist crumbs. Cool for 5 minutes, then move to a rack and cool completely.
  5. For the frosting, beat the butter for 2 minutes, then add the powdered sugar and cocoa in three parts, followed by the milk, vanilla and salt. Beat for 3 minutes until fluffy and pipe onto the cupcakes.

Baker's note: Use water instead of coffee for children; the cake will be a shade milder.

Red Velvet

A whisper of cocoa, a tang of buttermilk and that famous red, under a thick swirl of cream cheese frosting.

Makes 12 cupcakes · 175°C / 350°F · 18–20 min

Plain flour180 g
Cocoa powder10 g
Baking soda4 g
Fine salt2 g
White sugar190 g
Eggs, room temperature100 g
Vegetable oil120 g
Buttermilk120 g
Red gel food colouring8 g
Vanilla extract5 g
White vinegar5 g
Cream cheese frosting
Unsalted butter, soft115 g
Cream cheese, cold225 g
Powdered sugar, sifted300 g
Vanilla extract5 g
  1. Heat the oven and line a 12-hole muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. Whisk the flour, cocoa, baking soda and salt in a bowl.
  3. In another bowl whisk the sugar, eggs, oil, buttermilk, colouring, vanilla and vinegar until evenly red. Fold in the dry ingredients until just combined.
  4. Divide the batter between the liners, about 60 g each. Bake until the tops spring back. Cool for 5 minutes, then move to a rack and cool completely.
  5. For the frosting, beat the butter and powdered sugar until smooth, then add the cold cream cheese and vanilla and beat only until combined and fluffy. Pipe onto the cupcakes and keep them chilled.

Baker's note: Gel colouring gives a deep red without thinning the batter. Crumble one cupcake over the rest for the classic finish.

Confetti Birthday

Vanilla cake speckled with rainbow sprinkles and topped with a pale buttercream swirl. Instant birthday.

Makes 12 cupcakes · 175°C / 350°F · 18–20 min

Unsalted butter, soft115 g
White sugar200 g
Eggs, room temperature100 g
Vanilla extract6 g
Plain flour190 g
Baking powder7 g
Fine salt2 g
Rainbow sprinkles (jimmies)50 g
Milk, room temperature120 g
Vanilla buttercream
Unsalted butter, soft225 g
Powdered sugar, sifted360 g
Milk or cream30 g
Vanilla extract6 g
Sprinkles, to decoratea handful
Fine salta pinch
  1. Heat the oven and line a 12-hole muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar for 3 minutes until pale and fluffy. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla.
  3. Whisk the flour, baking powder and salt. Add to the bowl in three parts, alternating with the milk, mixing on low only until combined, then fold in the sprinkles with a few strokes.
  4. Divide the batter between the liners, about 65 g each. Bake until the tops spring back and a skewer comes out with moist crumbs. Cool for 5 minutes, then move to a rack and cool completely.
  5. For the buttercream, beat the butter for 2 minutes, add the powdered sugar in three parts, then the milk, flavouring and salt. Beat for 3 more minutes until light, then pipe a tall swirl on each cupcake. Finish with more sprinkles.

Baker's note: Use long jimmies, not round nonpareils; round ones bleed colour into the batter.

Linzer Cookies

Buttery almond and cinnamon cookies sandwiched with raspberry jam and dusted with snow. A classic from Austria.

Makes about 20 · 175°C / 350°F · 10–12 min

Unsalted butter, soft225 g
Powdered sugar100 g
Egg yolk20 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Lemon zest2 g
Almond flour100 g
Plain flour250 g
Ground cinnamon2 g
Fine salt2 g
Filling
Seedless raspberry jam150 g
Powdered sugar20 g
  1. Beat the butter and powdered sugar for 2 minutes, then beat in the yolk, vanilla and zest.
  2. Mix in the almond flour, flour, cinnamon and salt on low until a soft dough forms. Divide in two, flatten into discs, wrap and chill for 1 hour.
  3. Roll out 4 mm thick between two sheets of baking paper. Cut 6 cm rounds and cut a small window from the centre of half of them.
  4. Bake on lined trays until the edges are just golden. Cool completely.
  5. Dust the window tops with powdered sugar. Spread the solid bases with jam and press the tops on.

Baker's note: Dust the tops before sandwiching, so the jam stays glossy and red.

Pfeffernüsse

Little German spice cookies with honey, black pepper and anise, dipped in a crackly white glaze. Better after a few days in the tin.

Makes about 40 · 175°C / 350°F · 12–14 min

Unsalted butter60 g
Honey110 g
Molasses40 g
Dark brown sugar70 g
Egg50 g
Plain flour280 g
Almond flour30 g
Baking soda3 g
Ground cinnamon4 g
Ground ginger2 g
Ground cloves1 g
Ground nutmeg1 g
Black pepper1 g
Ground anise1 g
Fine salt1 g
Glaze
Powdered sugar200 g
Milk35 g
  1. Warm the butter, honey, molasses and sugar in a pan until melted. Cool for 10 minutes, then whisk in the egg.
  2. Stir in the flours, baking soda, spices and salt to make a sticky dough. Cover and chill for at least 2 hours.
  3. Roll into 15 g balls and bake on lined trays until firm and lightly cracked.
  4. Cool completely. Whisk the glaze until smooth and thick.
  5. Dip each cookie in the glaze, lift out with a fork and let it set on a rack for 1 hour.

Baker's note: Store them in a tin with a slice of apple for two days and they soften into the traditional texture.

Spritz Butter Cookies

Piped butter cookies in rings, swirls and stars. One dough, a cookie press and forty minutes to fill a tin.

Makes about 60 · 190°C / 375°F · 8–10 min

Unsalted butter, soft225 g
White sugar100 g
Egg yolk20 g
Vanilla extract5 g
Almond extract1 g
Plain flour280 g
Fine salt1 g
To decorate
Coloured sugar or sprinklesa few pinches
  1. Beat the butter and sugar for 3 minutes until pale and fluffy.
  2. Beat in the yolk, vanilla and almond extract.
  3. Mix in the flour and salt on low until just combined. The dough should be soft enough to pipe.
  4. Press or pipe shapes onto unlined, ungreased trays; the dough needs to grip the metal. Decorate with coloured sugar if you like.
  5. Bake until the edges are just golden. Cool for 5 minutes on the tray, then move to a rack.

Baker's note: If the dough is too stiff to pipe, warm the bag in your hands for a minute. If shapes spread, chill the piped trays for 10 minutes.

Walnut Apricot Rugelach

Flaky cream cheese pastry rolled around apricot jam, cinnamon sugar and walnuts. Crescent-shaped and dangerously easy to eat.

Makes 48 pieces · 175°C / 350°F · 20–22 min

Plain flour250 g
Fine salt2 g
Unsalted butter, cold, cubed225 g
Cream cheese, cold225 g
Filling
Light brown sugar100 g
Ground cinnamon4 g
Walnuts, finely chopped100 g
Apricot jam120 g
To finish
Egg, beaten50 g
Coarse sugar20 g
  1. Pulse the flour and salt with the butter and cream cheese in a food processor until the dough just clumps. Divide into 4, flatten into discs, wrap and chill for 2 hours.
  2. Mix the brown sugar, cinnamon and walnuts.
  3. Roll each disc into a 23 cm circle, spread thinly with jam and sprinkle with a quarter of the nut mixture. Cut into 12 wedges and roll each up from the wide end.
  4. Place on lined trays, brush with egg and sprinkle with coarse sugar. Chill for 15 minutes.
  5. Bake until deep golden. Cool on the tray for 10 minutes, as the jam is very hot.

Baker's note: Freeze shaped, unbaked rugelach on a tray, then bag them. Bake from frozen, adding 3 to 4 minutes.

Nan-e Nokhodchi (Chickpea Flour Cookies)

Tiny clover-shaped cookies of roasted chickpea flour and cardamom that dissolve on the tongue. No Nowruz tray is complete without them.

Makes about 40 · 150°C / 300°F · 20–25 min

Roasted chickpea flour250 g
Powdered sugar125 g
Ground cardamom3 g
Ghee or unsalted butter, soft125 g
Rosewater5 g
To decorate
Pistachio sliversa handful
  1. Whisk the chickpea flour, powdered sugar and cardamom together.
  2. Add the soft ghee and rosewater and knead for a full 10 minutes until the dough is smooth and slightly shiny. Wrap and chill for 1 hour.
  3. Pat or roll the dough 1.5 cm thick between two sheets of baking paper and cut small clovers.
  4. Press a sliver of pistachio into the centre of each and set them on a lined tray.
  5. Bake low until set but still pale. Cool completely on the tray; they are very fragile while warm.

Baker's note: Long kneading is the secret. Under-kneaded dough bakes into cookies that crumble instead of melting.

Nan-e Berenji (Rice Flour Cookies)

Snow-white rice flour cookies scented with rosewater and cardamom, topped with poppy seeds. A speciality of Qazvin.

Makes about 36 · 150°C / 300°F · 15–18 min

Ghee or unsalted butter, soft125 g
Powdered sugar120 g
Egg white30 g
Rosewater10 g
Ground cardamom2 g
Rice flour250 g
To decorate
Poppy seedsa pinch
  1. Beat the ghee and powdered sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes.
  2. Beat in the egg white, rosewater and cardamom.
  3. Mix in the rice flour to a soft dough. Wrap and chill for 1 hour.
  4. Roll 15 g balls, flatten slightly and press a pattern with the tines of a fork. Sprinkle with poppy seeds.
  5. Bake until set but completely white; they should not brown. Cool on the tray.

Baker's note: Use the finest rice flour you can find. Coarse rice flour makes a gritty cookie.

Nan-e Badami (Almond Cookies)

Chewy almond cookies with crisp edges, made with egg whites and no flour. Naturally gluten-free.

Makes about 30 · 165°C / 325°F · 15–18 min

Egg whites70 g
White sugar150 g
Almond flour200 g
Ground cardamom2 g
Rosewater5 g
To decorate
Almond sliversa handful
  1. Whisk the egg whites to soft peaks, then whisk in the sugar a spoonful at a time until glossy.
  2. Fold in the almond flour, cardamom and rosewater.
  3. Pipe or spoon small mounds onto lined trays and top each with a few almond slivers.
  4. Leave for 30 minutes to form a thin skin.
  5. Bake until pale golden at the edges. Cool fully before lifting.

Baker's note: The resting time forms the crackly top; don't skip it.

Nan-e Gerdooyi (Walnut Cookies)

Crisp-edged, chewy walnut cookies made with egg yolks. Simple, rich and a bakery classic.

Makes about 30 · 165°C / 325°F · 12–15 min

Egg yolks80 g
Powdered sugar100 g
Vanilla extract3 g
Walnuts, finely chopped200 g
  1. Whisk the yolks and powdered sugar for 5 minutes until very thick and pale.
  2. Whisk in the vanilla.
  3. Fold in the walnuts.
  4. Drop teaspoon-sized mounds onto lined trays, well apart.
  5. Bake until golden at the edges. Cool on the tray before lifting.

Baker's note: Chop the walnuts finely by hand; ground walnuts turn the batter oily.
